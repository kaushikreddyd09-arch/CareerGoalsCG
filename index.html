Reading the catalogue

Three kinds of eligibility

Every course carries one of three markers. They tell you whether the requirement is fixed, open, or set by the institution you apply to.

Subject required

Mandatory subjects

The course requires specific Class 12 subjects, and no institution can waive them. MBBS needs Biology, Physics and Chemistry. B.Tech needs Mathematics, Physics and Chemistry. If you did not take those subjects, the course is not open to you.

Open to all

No subject requirement

The course sets no specific Class 12 subject requirement, so any recognised stream can apply. Most language, humanities, law, management and vocational degrees sit here. Institutions may still set their own minimum marks.

Varies by institution

Set by the institution

The subject requirement genuinely differs between institutions and programmes. BCA is the clearest example: many institutions require Mathematics at Class 12, many do not. Always confirm with the institution before you rely on it.

On accuracy. These rules describe the standard national position for each degree. They are not a substitute for an institution's own published eligibility conditions, which can add minimum percentages, subject combinations, age limits or their own assessments. Where a course is marked varies, treat the institution's own statement as the only authoritative one.

What this is

One question, answered properly.

CareerGoals exists to answer exactly one thing: I studied this stream in Class 12 — what undergraduate courses can I pursue next? It is not a college finder, not an entrance-exam guide and not a counselling service. Everything on this page serves that single question.

  • Every course listed with its degree type, typical duration and the Class 12 subjects it requires.
  • No accounts, no sign-in, no personal information collected. The whole catalogue is open on arrival.
  • No colleges, rankings, entrance examinations, admission procedures, scholarships, fees or salary figures.
  • Eligibility rules are stated as they are — and marked clearly where they differ between institutions.
